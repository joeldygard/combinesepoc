## Fixed in the first pass

**The black↔burgundy cant seam.** `.pagehero` was clipped with
`polygon(0 0, 100% 0, 100% 100%, 0 calc(100% - 1.6vw))`. A clip makes the removed
wedge *transparent*, not coloured — so what showed through it was the `<body>`
background, i.e. white. Against the white field that follows on most pages that was
invisible. On two pages it was not:

- `/ai-for-the-physical-world/` — black page hero meets the plum stack field.
- `/insights/` — black page hero meets the black final CTA. This one was worse: a
  bright white blade between two black fields.

The same latent bug sat on the homepage `.hero` (2.5vw cant); it just happens to be
followed by a white section.

The cant is kept exactly as it was. The fix is that the *following* section is pulled
up by the cant depth and handed the same amount back as padding, so its own field
paints the wedge from below. Both heroes are `position: relative`, so they still paint
above the pulled-up background and the angled edge stays crisp.

```css
/* tokens.css — the depth is a token because the next section has to know it */
--cant-hero: 2.5vw;
--cant-page: 1.6vw;

/* Hero.css / Pages.css */
.pagehero + .section {
  margin-top: calc(-1 * var(--cant-page));
  padding-top: calc(var(--section-space) + var(--cant-page));
}
```

Verified: content top position is byte-identical before and after on both affected
pages at 1440 px and 390 px; document height unchanged. The homepage shifts down
exactly 1 px, because the now-redundant `margin-bottom: -1px` hairline hack on
`.hero` was removed with it.

## Findings, ranked by payoff

### 1. ~~Unblock the webfont stylesheet~~ — **FIXED**, measured −232 ms FCP

> Done. Both faces now use the `media="print"` swap, with a `<noscript>` fallback so a
> JS-off visitor still gets the real typefaces. On the identical shipped build the
> homepage went **644 ms → 412 ms** (median of 5), and the only render-blocking
> resource left is the site's own CSS. Self-hosting, described at the end of this
> section, is still the further step.

`index.html` preloads Host Grotesk *and then* loads it as a plain blocking
`<link rel="stylesheet">`. The preload doesn't make it non-blocking; it just warms the
fetch. Every first paint therefore waits on DNS + TCP + TLS + response from a
third-party origin. IBM Plex Mono two lines below already does this correctly with the
`media="print"` swap.

Median FCP over 5 runs, 4× CPU throttle, 9 Mbps / 60 ms RTT, 350 ms simulated
cold-origin latency for `fonts.googleapis.com`:

| Configuration | FCP |
|---|--:|
| As-is | **616 ms** |
| Fonts stylesheet made non-blocking | **348 ms** |
| No webfont link at all (theoretical floor) | 324 ms |

Applying the same `media="print" onload="this.media='all'"` treatment to Host Grotesk
lands within 24 ms of the floor. `display=swap` is already in the URL, so the fallback
stack renders immediately and the swap is invisible on a fast connection.

Better still: self-host both faces. It removes the third-party origin entirely, lets
you subset to Latin, and puts the font on the same connection as the CSS.

> On simulated Slow 3G the local CSS becomes the bottleneck and the gap narrows to
> ~20 ms — the win is real but it is a mid-and-fast-network win.

### 2. The header symbol is a 177 kB PNG

`SiteHeader` imports `assets/combine-symbol.png` (177 kB) and renders it at 1.6 rem —
about 26 px tall, from a 636×1024 source. `assets/combine-symbol.opt.png` (74 kB) is in
the repo, unused. Neither is the right answer at this size: the logotype next to it is
already inline SVG. Either inline the symbol as SVG too (zero requests, crisp at the
3.9 rem detached "coin" size), or ship a ~4 kB WebP.

It isn't render-blocking, so it doesn't move FCP directly — but it's 177 kB competing
for bandwidth on every page load, and it's the largest single asset in the build.

### 3. ~~`--text-max: 44rem` is sized for the lede, not for body copy~~ — **FIXED**

> Done. `--text-max-body: 36rem` added and applied to `.u-body`; measured 70 chars/line
> at every occurrence that was 86. The three component `max-width: 40rem` declarations
> that the type utilities had silently been overriding are gone, so measure is now
> declared once, on the type role.

Measured characters per line at 1440 px:

| Role | Size | Box | Chars/line | Verdict |
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| `.u-lede` | 21 px | 704 px | 70 | Good |
| `.u-body` | 17 px | 704 px | **86** | Too long |
| `.platform-copy__item` @1024 | 17 px | 282 px | **34** | Too short |

One token is doing two jobs. 44 rem is right at 21 px and about 8 rem too wide at
17 px. Suggest splitting: `--text-max: 44rem` for lede, `--text-max-body: 36rem`
(≈70 chars at `--step-0`), applied on `.u-body`. The hero body paragraph and
`proof-grid__foot` are the visible offenders on the homepage.

### 4. Three grids break to multi-column at 42 rem (672 px)

`.content-grid--3`, `.project-grid--teasers` and `.stack-list__row` all switch at the
same `@media (min-width: 42rem)`. At 672 px that gives:

- Three project cards ~180 px wide with 2 rem padding → ~116 px of usable text width,
  inside a 22 rem-tall card.
- Stack rows squeezing "Model predictive control, state estimation, adaptive control"
  into roughly 250 px.
- `platform-copy` at 34 chars/line by 1024 px.

42 rem is a two-column breakpoint. Three-column layouts want ~60 rem. Recommend
keeping 42 rem for `--2` grids and moving every three-column rule to 60 rem.

### 5. ~~The project cards aren't links~~ — **FIXED**

> Done, architecture-first. `cases.ts` held three finished case studies that nothing
> imported; they now drive both the grid and three real `/projects/<slug>/` routes.
> The whole card is the click target through a single anchor on the title, and the
> ACHT entry — public client, no write-up — stays deliberately unlinked because
> `hasPage: false` makes that a type-level fact rather than an oversight.

Confirmed in the prerendered HTML: none of the `article.project-card` elements contain
an anchor. Every user will click them. It also means the homepage emits exactly one
link to `/projects/` and zero links to any individual project — there is no internal
link graph below the top level.

### 6. ~~The homepage renders the same "Services" link four times~~ — **FIXED**

> Done. `link` moved out of the `ContentBlocks` map, which is what the rest of the site
> already did. The homepage now emits one `Services` anchor and three distinct project
> links.

`ContentBlocks` puts the `link` prop inside the `.map()`, so all four entry-point
blocks get their own identical `Services → /services/` button. Link counts from
`dist/index.html`:

```
4 x  /services/                   'Services'
2 x  /contact/                    'Talk to an engineer'
1 x  /projects/                   'See our projects'
1 x  /ai-for-the-physical-world/  'How we build'
1 x  /projects/                   'See all projects'
1 x  /insights/                   'Read our engineering notes'
1 x  mailto:contact@combine.se    'contact@combine.se'
```

Four anchors with the same accessible name and destination, in a row. Move `link`
outside the map so the section gets one.

### 7. Four routes are thin enough to hurt

`/insights/` (40 words), `/contact/` (32), `/company/` (47), `/products/` (81). All four
are in the nav, in `sitemap.xml`, and prerendered as indexable HTML. `/insights/` is the
worst case: it's the destination of an RSS feed containing zero `<item>`s, and the
secondary CTA on every other page ("Read our engineering notes") points at it.

Cheapest fix: `noindex` the empty ones and drop them from the sitemap until they have
content, and repoint the CTA. `src/content/articles.ts` and `blogPostingSchema()`
already exist and are unused — the plumbing for real Insights content is built.

### 8. `og:image` points at a file that isn't there

`index.html` declares `https://combine.se/social/combine-default.png` at 1200×630, and
`public/` contains no `social/` directory. Every LinkedIn, Slack and X share of any page
renders without an image. Also missing: `og:image:alt`. `twitter:card` is set to
`summary_large_image`, which without a working image degrades to no card at all.

### 9. Smaller items

- **`<ol class="stack-list">` with `list-style: none`.** Safari + VoiceOver drop list
  semantics when `list-style` is `none`. Add `role="list"`. The reset in `global.css`
  strips it site-wide, so this applies to the footer and nav lists too.
- **Footer nav labels are `<h2>`.** "Work with us" / "Company" / "Contact" are 14 px
  uppercase mono nav labels rendered as second-level headings on all 8 pages. They
  outrank real section content in the outline. `<p>` inside the already-`aria-label`ed
  `<nav>` is enough.
- **Sections with no heading.** The proof-metrics section and the two `SectionLabel`
  sections on `/services/` use a `<p class="u-eyebrow">` where a heading belongs, so
  `aria-label` is carrying the whole burden.
- **`new Date().getFullYear()` in `SiteFooter`.** Prerendered at build time, recomputed
  at hydration — React will warn about a text mismatch on any page built in one year
  and viewed in the next. Bake the year at build time or read it from `site`.
- **`<dl>` in `column-reverse`.** Value renders above label; DOM and screen-reader order
  are label-then-value. Reversing the DOM and dropping `column-reverse` matches both.
- **`breadcrumbSchema()` is written and unused.** Adding `BreadcrumbList` to the seven
  non-home routes is a ~10-line change to the `routeShells` plugin.
- **`sitemap.xml` has no `<lastmod>`** and is hand-maintained in `public/` while the
  route table lives in `src/content/site.ts`. It's already in sync — it won't stay that
  way. Generate it in `vite.config.ts` the way JSON-LD already is.
- **`_blank` links carry no "opens in new window" cue** (Career in the nav, Sympathy on
  `/products/`).
- **Contrast floor is 4.53:1** (`--color-text-secondary-on-subtle` on `--color-paper`,
  21 px). That passes AA and the tokens file shows the maths was done deliberately —
  worth knowing it's the floor, and that 21 px/400 does *not* qualify as WCAG large text.
- **`DriftHero` runs `build()` + `renderOnce()` synchronously in its mount effect.** No
  FCP cost (the hero text is prerendered), but it's a chunk of main-thread work
  immediately after hydration — it shows up in TBT/INP, not FCP. Worth an
  `requestIdleCallback` or a first-frame deferral if interaction latency ever matters.
- **`DriftHero`'s canvas paints `#000000` while `.hero` is `#141414`.** Invisible in
  practice; the canvas covers the field. Flagging only so it doesn't surprise anyone
  during the fade-out transition.

## What's genuinely good

Worth saying, because the failures above are all fixable and the foundations are not
the kind of thing you can retrofit:

- **Real prerendered HTML.** `entry-server.tsx` + `scripts/prerender.mjs` means every
  route ships complete markup. FCP does not wait on 72 kB of JavaScript, and crawlers
  that don't execute JS see the whole page. This is the single biggest thing the site
  does right.
- **Metadata is generated, not pasted.** `routeShells()` derives title, description,
  canonical and OG from the same `routes` table the router uses; `jsonLd()` derives
  Organization and WebSite schemas from `site.ts`. Structured data cannot drift from
  content, which is the usual failure mode.
- **Tokens are enforced.** No raw colour or type size anywhere outside `tokens.css`, and
  the two-tier secondary-text tokens exist specifically because 4.27:1 on paper failed
  AA. Someone measured.
- **Progressive enhancement is real.** `Link` is an actual anchor with modifier-key and
  new-tab behaviour intact; the mobile menu is in the DOM and toggled with `hidden`, so
  navigation works with JS off.
- **Dead code is tree-shaken.** `DriftField`, `DialsLab`, `DriftControls` and
  `CaseStandin` — roughly 2,000 lines — are confirmed absent from the shipped bundle.
